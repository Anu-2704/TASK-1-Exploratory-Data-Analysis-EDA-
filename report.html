Key Findings & Patterns

[OK]Heavily skewed ratings: 79.8% of all reviews are 5-star. The distribution is strongly right-skewed with a very small proportion of 1–3 star reviews (9.9% combined).
[OK]Negative reviewers write more: 1-star reviews average 559 chars, 2-star 623 chars, while 5-star reviews average only 234 chars. All pairwise differences are statistically significant (p < 0.0001).
[OK]Helpfulness is sparse and binary: Only 11.3% of reviews received any votes. Of those, the median helpful ratio is 1.0 — reviews tend to be either all-helpful or none-helpful.
[OK]Longer reviews attract more votes: Pearson r = 0.45 between review_length and total_vote. Longer reviews are significantly more likely to be voted on.
[OK]Review volume peaked in 2013: 2,678 reviews in 2013 vs 501 in 2012 and 1,736 in 2014, suggesting the product reached peak popularity mid-lifecycle.
[OK]Slight temporal rating decline: Reviews with smallest day_diff (newest relative to reference) rate 4.70 on average, while oldest quartile rates 4.45 (r = −0.09).
[OK]Outliers present: The top review received 2,020 total votes (8,638 chars). These power-user reviews should be treated separately or log-transformed in modelling.
[OK]Data quality is excellent: Only 1 missing reviewerName and 1 missing reviewText. Zero duplicate rows or reviewerIDs across 4,915 records.
